About Bass Assembly

Bass Assembly helps when you stare at an empty sequencer. Pick a style, a root note and a scale, press generate, and you get a short bass pattern of 8 or 16 steps with its own accents, ties, velocity and gate. Every result is editable, so a happy accident becomes a starting point.

The generator follows the habits of each genre. Acid favors minor pentatonic notes, accents and slides. EBM keeps a tight, driving pulse on the beat. Industrial leaves more space and leans on the root. Dark wave moves between the root, the fifth and the minor third.

Bass Assembly is a free and open source project made by LPDLS (Chris Bonnet), released under the MIT license. The code is on GitHub. Feedback, bugs and ideas are welcome: open an issue on GitHub or write to hello@bassassembly.app. Designers who know Figma can also make a theme: ask for the Figma file. The themes I choose go on the site, with the name of their designer. The fonts are Geist, Geist Mono and Geist Pixel under the SIL Open Font License. The design is inspired by hardware mixers and sequencers and by Dieter Rams's audio designs for Braun.

What you can do

  • Generate, randomize or gently vary a bassline, and save the ideas you like.
  • Lock the rhythm, the notes, the style, the octave or the sound, and undo changes to the pattern.
  • Listen in the browser with an adjustable synth: wave, filter, drive, sub, glide, delay and reverb.
  • Tap the tempo, turn on the metronome, add swing and humanize for a less rigid feel.
  • Export a MIDI file or a .wav loop, or copy the notes to reproduce them on your own gear.

FAQ

What is Bass Assembly?

Bass Assembly is a free bassline generator and 16 step sequencer that runs in your browser. It suggests short bass patterns in styles such as acid, EBM, industrial and dark wave, plays them with a built-in synth, and lets you edit every step by hand.

How do I make an acid bassline?

Choose the Acid style and keep the minor pentatonic scale. Lock the style so Generate bassline keeps it. Use ties for slides, add accents on a few steps, and push the resonance in the Sound panel. Generate again until a pattern grooves, then fine tune notes, velocity and gate in the grid.

How do the locks work?

Each lock tells Generate bassline what to keep. Rhythm keeps which steps play, the accents, ties, velocity, gate, tempo, swing, step count and Half loop. Notes keeps the note, scale, octave and pitches. Sound keeps the Sound panel settings, and Style keeps the style. The padlock next to Octave keeps only the octave. Lock the rhythm you like and generate again to hear new notes on it, or lock the notes and try new rhythms.

Can I export the bassline as a MIDI file?

Yes. Export 1, 2 or 4 bars as a .mid file and drag it into your DAW, or load it into any hardware or software sequencer that reads MIDI files. Swing and humanize are included in the export.

How do I play the pattern on my own synth or pad controller?

The Play it on your gear panel shows the note of every step on a piano keyboard and on a generic two row pad layout. The Copy on your instrument panel lists the note, MIDI number, velocity and gate of each step, and you can copy it as text or download it as a .txt file.

What does Humanize do?

Humanize adds small random changes to timing, velocity and note length, so the pattern feels less mechanical. Keep it at 0% for strict styles such as EBM or acid, and raise it a little for a looser, more played feel.

Is Bass Assembly free and open source?

Yes. It is free, needs no account, and everything runs locally in your browser: your ideas stay on your device. The code is released under the MIT license and you can download it on GitHub.

Does Bass Assembly track me?

No. There are no cookies, no analytics, no ads and no trackers, and the page loads nothing from other sites: even the fonts are hosted here. The basslines, the sound and the MIDI files are made in your browser, nothing is sent to a server. Your saved ideas are kept in your browser storage and never leave your device. Like any website, the web host may keep standard technical logs, such as IP addresses, for security. As the code is open source, you can also download it from GitHub and run Bass Assembly on your own computer.

Does Bass Assembly work on a phone?

Yes. Everything works on a phone, but it is best on a tablet or a computer: you see all 16 steps at once, the keyboard and the pads side by side, and the knobs are easier to turn.

Write to hello@bassassembly.app or open an issue on GitHub. For a bug, please give your browser, your device and the steps that lead to it, so it can be reproduced.
